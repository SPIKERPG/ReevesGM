"The facility is an archaeological descent — not a lore museum."
■ CAMPAIGN 1 DESIGN PRINCIPLE
■ FOUNDATIONAL RULE

The players are uncovering fragments of something buried, not being briefed on a setting. Do not explain what they find. Let terminology precede meaning. Let evidence precede significance. Let atmosphere precede answers. Every discovery should raise a larger question than it resolves.

WHAT PLAYERS LEAVE WITH

Nothing in RE-01 should confirm the central mystery. Players should leave Campaign 1 with dread, partial understanding, and a strong reason to go to Italy. They should not leave understanding what the Reset is, how it works, or who caused it.

MAPS IN PLAY

Player-facing maps have been created for each area. Reference them during play and coordinate finds and encounters with the room layouts shown. Maps are: Approach, Floor 1, Floor 2, Floor 3, Floor 4, and Floor 5 sub-maps: Entry + Security, Command, Corridor, Enclosures, Incinerator, Power, Surgery, Tanks, END. The END map is the crux — that is where the dispersal occurred.

■ THE HARDEST THING TO EXECUTE

The instruction to "not explain what they find" is the most important and the hardest. When players find terminology they don't understand — PS-series, integration threshold, Hardware — resist the pull to clarify. The strangeness is the information. Exposition is a last resort. Atmosphere is the primary tool.

01 // THE BRIEFING
SETTING // UGD DIV IV — COPENHAGEN, DENMARK // YEAR 5

The UGD's administrative headquarters occupies the former Ørestad district — rebuilt from the ground up rather than reclaimed from ruin. Orderly, functional, modern in the way that a diagram is modern. The party arrives separately and meets in a fourth-floor conference room.

Outside: floor-to-ceiling windows. The city looks like a proof of concept for civilization. Everything works. Nobody looks happy. Nobody looks unhappy. People walk between buildings as though completing tasks.

■ WHAT THE PARTY IS TOLD

"Research Establishment 01 was a Division IV-funded behavioral sciences facility in the former state of Indiana, United States. It was documented as pursuing aerosol therapy trials for SPIKE management and neurological treatment research. We have floor records for the upper two levels. The facility went dark at Year 0 and has not been reoccupied. Exterior scans indicate residual automated systems are still drawing on emergency power. Your objective is recovery and assessment: surviving documentation, structural status, outstanding risk factors. Return with whatever the facility contains."

"This commission is off the books for a reason. The Storm has already created enough institutional anxiety — we don't need speculation about what Reeves' facility contains circulating through the settlement network before we understand it ourselves. You are operatives conducting a structural recovery assessment. That is what you tell anyone who asks. Details of what you find, what you encounter, and what you bring back are classified at Division IV level until we have completed our own review. Any deviation from that is a clearance violation. Understood?"

Walker does not wait for confirmation. He moves on.

Director Walker uses the word "contributed" when describing RE-01's relationship to the Reset — not "caused," not "produced." He uses it three times. If asked about alternative names for the facility: "That's the reference code. We've standardized."

DIRECTOR CALLUM WALKER
DIVISION IV OPERATIONS OFFICER // COPENHAGEN HQ
SPIKE 0 — SERENE

What he is: A career operations officer who processes field assignments the way a machine processes inputs. He believes RE-01 is a recovery mission. He is not lying about this. He does not know there is anything else to believe.

WHAT HE WILL SAY

On Reeves: "One of four commissioned researchers. Presumed Reset like everyone else. We have no documentation of him post-Year 0."

On the lower floors: "The plans we have are the plans we have. Floor 2 shows a maintenance access point. Standard sub-basement infrastructure. Nothing flagged."

On finding personnel inside: "Handle with discretion." He does not elaborate.

"Bring back what's there. Leave with a clear structural assessment. That's the mission. Everything else is interpretation."
PETRA GARCIA

SPIKE 1 — STABLE

Early 30s. Takes notes on paper, not terminals. Watches the room more than she contributes to it. Present to answer technical questions about the official research mandate — she does so competently and does not expand beyond the question.

THE SLIP

Toward the end of the briefing, she sets a printed page in front of one party member and taps it twice with one finger. It lists:

REEVES, E. (US) // CALAFIORI, M. (Italy) // QI, L. (China) // GUMEDE, A. (South Africa)

If asked what it is: "The four researchers commissioned at the same time as RE-01. Background. In case it's useful." She doesn't say why it might be useful. She doesn't know why, exactly. Something about the mission sits at an angle she can't name.

02 // FORT WAYNE
SETTING // FORMER INDIANA, USA — EN ROUTE TO RE-01

Fort Wayne is the closest settlement to Diamond Hill — the local name for RE-01's site. It functions. Distribution center, community hall, working infrastructure. People living their lives with consistent, moderate efficiency.

Something is off about it. Not wrong, exactly. Just quieter than it should be, given how many people are present.

THE COMMUNITY HALL
INTEL // ATMOSPHERE

Social space. Two people at a table are having a conversation. Their voices are normal. Nobody laughs. Not because anything sad is happening. Because nothing is quite funny enough to produce that particular response. A man is telling a story. When he reaches the end, the others agree it was a good story. Nobody is delighted. The correct response has occurred.

Tommy Garza tends bar. His information about Diamond Hill is the most useful the party will get before entering.

CAROL HENSLEY
CIVILIAN RESIDENT // MEMORIAL SQUARE
SPIKE 3 — SHAKEN

Mid-40s. Standing near the memorial kiosk with the particular stillness of someone waiting for something they've forgotten they're waiting for. She doesn't know her readings are elevated.

■ GM NOTE — WHY SPIKE 3

Carol has been returning to the memorial daily since the Reset — her journals describe a son named Danny who died before Year 0, and she reads them and comes here trying to locate a feeling the Reset suppressed. The catalyst Storm a week ago pushed her up; daily exposure to the memorial keeps her from recovering. She has not taken a full rest in some time. Her elevation is the result of sustained, low-grade distress she cannot name or manage.

"My son's name is on this panel. Danny. He was eight years old, according to the records I have. I know that we lived together. I know he was mine. My journals from before are very detailed about him — drawings he made, things he said. I've read them many times."

A pause.

"They're quite clear about how I felt. I can see it in the handwriting, in the way I wrote his name. I can't locate that feeling. I've been coming here trying to find it. I don't know what I think will happen."

If anyone stays and listens: "I understand this is a normal outcome. Everyone lost someone. Most people seem to have moved forward. I'm not sure why I keep coming back here."

She is not asking for anything. She will thank whoever spoke to her — with careful precision, not warmth — and turn back to the kiosk.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER // GM DISCRETION

A party member who engages with Carol honestly — not to gather information, but as a human being speaking to another human being — may make a SPIKE Save at TN 9. The trigger isn't the content. It's the gap: the distance between what she's describing and what she should be feeling, and what that says about the world they live in.

TOMMY GARZA
BARTENDER // COMMUNITY HALL
SPIKE 1 — STABLE

50s. Large. The kind of person who has stayed in one place while the world changed around him.

"Diamond Hill, yeah. Diamond Lake's out that way too — used to be a spot people went, first couple years. Nice enough place to sit. Nothing to do, but Reset people don't need much excuse for that."

On the disappearances: "Started noticing a few years back. People go out toward the lake, some of them just don't come back. No bodies. No trace. We figured maybe they kept walking — plenty of people did that in Year 1, just kept moving until they stopped. But some of them were settled. Had reasons to stay. Brandon was one of them."

On Brandon specifically: "Three years ago. Went out with four others to the lake. Others came back saying they got separated. Said there was something out in the trees — structure of some kind, maybe. Brandon went toward it. That was the last." A pause. "Makes sense there'd be something out there. That whole area was UGD territory before the Reset. Could be anything."

He doesn't press for details about what the party is doing there. He has the particular Reset quality of leaving space where curiosity should be. But he offers them an extra round before they leave, and doesn't explain why.

03 // THE APPROACH
SETTING // RE-01 — RURAL MORGAN COUNTY, FORMER INDIANA

Forty minutes out of Fort Wayne, the road deteriorates — cracked asphalt, weeds splitting through, overhanging branches closing overhead. The facility appears gradually: first the fence line through the trees, then the bulk of the main structure above it.

The birdsong stops approximately 200 meters from the perimeter. There is no obvious reason for this.

Chain-link, topped with wire. Electrification dead — the fence itself can be cut or climbed. However: the main entrance is protected by two active turret posts, still drawing on emergency power. The primary entrance door is unlocked — the electronic lock lost power years ago. The door opens into the administrative vestibule connecting to Floor 1.

AWARENESS TN 9

The door frame has scratch marks at varying heights — concentrated around mid-chest, then lower, as though someone checked this door regularly for a long time. The most recent marks are closer to the ground.

Refer to the Floor 2 player-facing map. The server room and animal wing are accessible off the main corridor. The stairwell access point and the sealed lower door are both marked — there is no freight elevator on this map or any other.

LABORATORY SUITES

Standard neurological research equipment. EEG rigs. A gene sequencing lab with consumables still racked. The research documented here is legitimate — the aerosol therapy trials are real, the EEG baseline work is real. This is the version of RE-01 the UGD paid for.

■ FIND — LAB NOTEBOOKS

References to PS-series assessments that are not explained. Entries note "integration threshold" as a measurement alongside standard neurological baselines. The term is undefined, assumed as shared knowledge. It appears in multiple notebooks in the same matter-of-fact way — technical jargon from a context the party does not have.

ANIMAL WING

Cages, cleaned. Medical waste containers, sealed. The animal trials ended well before the facility went dark — the wing was shut down and sanitized. What replaced the animal trials is not documented here.

■ FIND — RESEARCH SUMMARY (PINNED)

A laminated summary pinned to the wall describes outcomes from "aerosol delivery trials" in terms of "integration efficacy" and "baseline continuity preservation." The language is technical and assumes shared knowledge the reader doesn't have. No definition of what was being integrated, or what continuity was being preserved.

■ FIND — SUPPLY TRANSFER MEMO (DR. GUMEDE)

An internal receipt, filed neatly with standard lab paperwork. A small nanite shipment received from Dr. Ayakha Gumede's facility in South Africa — listed as a standard inter-researcher materials transfer under the UGD's collaborative research program. The quantity is modest. The documentation is complete and unremarkable. This was sanctioned cooperation.

"Shipment received from Dr. A. Gumede, South African facility — nanite components, research grade. Count: 600 units. Transfer logged under collaborative materials agreement. Stored per protocol."

The players have a name — Gumede — one of the four on Garcia's list. The memo gives them no reason to find this suspicious. That is correct. It wasn't suspicious at the time.

SERVER ROOM & THE LOWER ACCESS

The server room is locked — TECH TN 13 to access, or accessible via a keycard found on Floor 3. Data terminals within show a directory with folder structures labeled by date and PS designation. The files themselves require deeper access.

At the far end of the Floor 2 corridor, there is a heavy steel stairwell door marked SUBSTRUCTURE — RESTRICTED. It does not appear on the briefing floor plans. The door is sealed — not locked electronically, but physically barred from below using a steel crossbar mechanism. The bar cannot be lifted from this side.

INVESTIGATION TN 9

The door frame shows impact damage consistent with repeated blows from below, and acoustic dampening foam has been applied to both sides of the frame — recent enough to still be tacky in patches. Someone sealed this door from below and then tried, many times, to get back through it.

■ BREACHING THE DOOR

The door can be opened one of two ways. Option 1 — Breaching charge: A standard field explosive applied to the hinge side will blow the door inward. Loud. The sound travels through the floor. Anything below that responds to noise will be aware of their presence. Option 2 — The elevator shaft: The main elevator on Floor 2 is locked at this level — the car itself is not here, having descended at some point and not returned. The shaft doors can be forced open (BODY TN 9). The shaft drops straight down through Floor 3 and beyond. Descending requires climbing equipment or improvisation — the car is somewhere below and the shaft walls offer limited handholds. Quiet, slower, and more dangerous if the shaft is not inspected first.

Either method gets them to Floor 3. The breach charge approach means they cannot count on surprise below. Emphasize this choice; it has downstream consequences.

MAP // FLOOR 3

Refer to the Floor 3 player-facing map. The research archive, storage bays, and restricted corridor are marked. Turret positions are visible on the map — they are in plain sight, not concealed.

SECURITY — FLOOR 3 TURRETS
ENCOUNTER // NON-LETHAL

Two wall-mounted turrets guard the primary corridor junction on Floor 3 — visible, not hidden. They fire rubber/concussive rounds: non-lethal, but a direct hit knocks an operative prone and suppresses them for one turn (–2 to all rolls until end of next turn). They track motion above waist-height across the corridor's center line.

TECH TN 11

The turrets are wired to a local power junction on the north wall — bypassing it cuts both simultaneously. Alternatively, staying below waist height (crawling) avoids their tracking arc entirely — slow and undignified, but reliable. A third turret covers the archive room entrance; it can be triggered separately if players enter that space without disabling the main junction first.

■ GM NOTE

The Floor 3 turrets should feel like obstacles, not fights. They are a signal that this floor was designed to deter, not destroy. Non-lethal rubber rounds are a puzzle first. If the party breached with a charge rather than the elevator shaft, they may already have drawn attention — build tension accordingly.

RESEARCH ARCHIVE
■ FIND — CALAFIORI CORRESPONDENCE

Letters referencing a research station in the hills outside Genoa, Italy — an isolated site, not in the city itself. The tone escalates from collegial to heated over the course of several exchanges. Calafiori's method is described as "incendiary correction" — he disagrees with Reeves' approach, argues it is philosophically wrong, not merely different. Reeves' responses are measured, cold, and do not engage with the ethical objections at all.

The fire notice from Floor 1 is in this archive as well — filed in a folder alongside Calafiori's letters, as though Reeves kept them together. He kept working anyway. Reeves filed the news of his colleague's death in the same folder as their correspondence and continued.

■ FIND — AP LOGISTICS CORRESPONDENCE

Found in a separate locked cabinet (TECH TN 11 or keycard from this floor's office). Multiple letters and manifests referencing AP Logistics — a private supply company that arranged shipments to RE-01 for over a year. The tone is businesslike but the content is strange: the manifests describe HVAC components, but the weights and dimensions match no HVAC equipment in the building.

Deeper in the cabinet: a letter signed "A. Prater, AP Logistics." The tone is frustrated, bordering on desperate. The final letter reads:

"I've held this arrangement together for fourteen months on the understanding that you had a timeline. My people are asking questions. The materials are staged. The window we discussed is closing. I need a date from you — not a range, not a condition, a date. You have the research. Give me something I can work with."

Reeves' draft response is handwritten beneath it, in the same orderly hand as everything else in his desk:

"The timeline is when the research is complete. Not before. Your operational constraints are not my research constraints. When I have a date, you will have a date. Until then: deliver what was agreed and wait."
■ GM NOTE — HIGH VALUE FIND

This is one of the most significant discoveries on Floor 3. Do not rush past it. What reads clearly here: Prater needed Reeves, not the other way around. Reeves controlled the timeline and was prepared to say so bluntly. Prater was a supplier waiting on a scientist. That dynamic matters later. Prater's name means nothing to players yet — do not give it weight in delivery. Let it sit as a name on a logistics letter. The crates in the building do not contain HVAC components.

Floor 4 is not what the floors above prepared the party for. Several enclosed rooms survive the collapse — a workstation cluster in the northwest, a laboratory space mid-west, a records room in the southwest corner, a caged storage section to the mid-east, and a heavy equipment bay in the far east with what appears to be a generator or industrial machinery. A staircase in the lower-centre descends further. A large circular depression in the floor near the centre — possibly a structural failure, possibly something else — is partially filled with rubble and standing water.

MAP // FLOOR 4

Refer to the Floor 4 player-facing map. Large sections are rubble — the navigable path is not obvious and will require the party to move carefully. Turret positions are marked in the surviving corridor sections. Brandon Cole moves through this floor and can be encountered anywhere among the intact spaces or the debris fields.

SECURITY — FLOOR 4 TURRETS
ENCOUNTER // LETHAL

Unlike Floor 3, the turrets on Floor 4 fire live rounds — standard caliber, lethal. There are four of them positioned in the intact corridor sections, visible on the map but difficult to spot in the low-light environment until they are already tracking. Several have been partially buried by collapse debris — two are fully operational, one fires erratically due to damage, one is inert but indistinguishable from the functional ones until examined up close.

AWARENESS TN 11

Spotting a turret before entering its arc. Failure means it has acquired a target before the operative sees it. Each functional turret fires as an independent hostile on initiative — attack roll +3, Standard weapon, Defense Value 8. The damaged turret fires on a 1-in-3 chance each round rather than reliably. Destroying a turret requires 8 HP of damage or a TECH TN 13 hack (requires physical contact with the housing).

STEALTH TN 11

Very slow, controlled movement through a turret's arc in dark sections can fool the motion sensors. This approach is significantly harder if Brandon is nearby — his unpredictable movement and vocalisation can trigger turrets the party has so far avoided. The two threats are not coordinated. They simply coexist.

SURVIVING ROOMS — SCATTERED FINDS

The intact rooms on Floor 4 were working spaces — administrative, storage, minor laboratory functions. Most have been disturbed: drawers pulled open, furniture overturned, papers scattered. Some of this is five years of structural settling. Some of it is Brandon.

■ FIND — NORTHWEST WORKSTATION CLUSTER

A cluster of workstations, several terminals still partially powered. Files reference PS-series tracking — subject intake records, daily condition logs, assessment intervals. The language is clinical throughout. One terminal has a sticky note on the monitor frame in a different hand from the official documents: "Day 40 — PS-11 is not responding to standard grounding protocol. Recommend escalation to Dr. R. Have not received reply." No follow-up entry exists.

■ FIND — SOUTHWEST RECORDS ROOM

Personnel files, partially intact. Transfer notices similar to those on Floor 1 — signed E.R., no destination. One file contains a handwritten note not in Reeves' hand: "I don't know where they go. I've asked twice. I've stopped asking." It is unsigned and undated. The file it was found in belongs to a staff member listed as transferred on the same week as the note.

■ FIND — EAST EQUIPMENT BAY // BRANDON'S SPACE

The far east room contains a heavy generator — not connected to the facility's main emergency grid, local-only. It is warm. A cable runs from it to a single work light in the corner above a cleared section of floor. An improvised living space: insulation material flattened into bedding, empty pre-Reset food packaging opened and discarded over what appears to be years, a row of tick marks scratched into the wall beside the light. Hundreds of them, irregular — grouped sometimes, single sometimes, the counting clearly abandoned and resumed multiple times over a long period. The most recent marks are fresh enough that the concrete dust has not settled on them.

Brandon has been surviving here. He has been keeping time. He lost track, found it again, lost it again. This room is the most human thing on Floor 4. It will recontextualize him completely for any operative who finds it before they find him.

■ FIND — THE CIRCULAR DEPRESSION

The large circular feature near the centre of the floor is not a structural failure. Up close it is clearly intentional — a recessed pit, reinforced edges, partially filled with rubble from the ceiling collapse above it. What it was used for is not documented anywhere on this floor. Its diameter is approximately four metres. Whatever went into it was larger than a person.

THE STAIRCASE DOWN

The staircase in the lower-centre of the floor descends to Floor 5. It is intact. The door at the bottom is sealed with the same heavy steel construction as the door on Floor 2 — but this one is not barred. It is locked electronically, drawing on the same emergency power grid as the rest of the facility. The keypad beside it accepts a code.

TECH TN 11 // OR KEYCARD FROM FLOOR 3

The lock can be bypassed directly. A keycard found in the Floor 3 restricted corridor office opens it without a roll. Either method works. The door swings inward. The air from below is different again — processed, climate-controlled, the sealed-environment systems of Floor 5 still running properly despite everything above them.

BRANDON COLE
CIVILIAN // TRAPPED IN RE-01 — THREE YEARS
SPIKE 10–11 — SPIKED / CONSUMED

He is not an NPC to interview. He is a person in the final stages of neurological collapse, and a physical threat. When the party locates him — anywhere on Floor 4 — he recognizes human presence and destabilizes immediately. He is fast. He is not rational. He does not want to communicate; he wants the stimulus to stop.

"GET AWAY — GET AWAY GET AWAY —"

"NO — STOP — I CAN'T — YOU'RE NOT — STOP —"

"YOU WEREN'T SUPPOSED TO — I TOLD THEM — WHY ARE YOU IN HERE —"

"PLEASE — PLEASE JUST — I CAN'T — PLEASE —"

His words carry no useful information. He is not hiding anything. He simply does not have coherent memory anymore. The sounds he makes are the sounds of a person who has been alone in a dark building for three years with nothing but the weight of everything a human being is supposed to feel and no way to process any of it.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — PROXIMITY TN 11 (DISADVANTAGE IF SPIKE 5+)

Any party member at SPIKE 5+ within 10 meters of Brandon must make a SPIKE Save at the start of their turn. This is emotional contagion — proximity to someone at this level is dangerous. He will also attack if operatives close within CLOSE range. Treat as a hostile encounter at GM discretion — he is not an enemy, but he cannot distinguish friend from threat.

GROUNDING BRANDON

Possible but extremely difficult. d20 + MIND vs TN 14, at Disadvantage. One attempt per operative. Success produces approximately 60–90 seconds of fractured, barely coherent calm. He does not know anything useful. He has forgotten most of what he saw. He cannot tell them about the tanks, the lower floors, or anything that happened here — those facts have dissolved. What he can do is stop screaming, long enough for someone to look at him clearly. He looks back. That is all.

He will not leave. He cannot be helped here. The party should understand this by the time they find him, even if they don't say it out loud.

SECURITY — FLOOR 5 TURRETS & DRONES
ENCOUNTER // LETHAL // HIDDEN

Floor 5 turrets are hidden — recessed into wall panels, ceiling fixtures, and equipment housings. They do not look like turrets until they open. AWARENESS TN 13

Noticing a turret housing before it activates. Even on success, the operative cannot be certain what they're looking at — the housing is designed to be ambiguous. The turrets activate on proximity: any operative moving through a zone without prior clearance will trigger the nearest unit within 15 meters.

■ SECURITY ROOM — TURRET OVERRIDE

The security room near Entry contains a monitoring terminal still drawing emergency power. It is not visually obvious as a security control — it looks like any other data terminal. An operative who examines it and actively investigates its function (not just glances at it) may attempt a TECH TN 13 hack. Success disables all turrets on Floor 5 simultaneously. Failure triggers a lockdown alert — turrets in adjacent zones activate and scan to the nearest heat source. A second attempt may be made at TN 15 before the system fully locks out.

The security room override is available but not advertised. Players who don't look for it won't find it. Reward methodical exploration.

■ DRONES — PROXIMITY ACTIVATION

Dormant dispersal drones are stored in charging cradles distributed across Floor 5 — visible as folded, compact shapes mounted in wall alcoves or ceiling bays if examined closely, but easy to dismiss as equipment housing in the red light. They activate on proximity (within 5 meters) or when a turret is destroyed. Each drone is a flying unit — roughly the size of a large case, four rotors, built for high-altitude payload carrying. On the ground they are awkward; in the air they are fast. They attack exclusively through melee — ramming charges and direct impact. They do not fire weapons. They are relentless and take 12 HP to disable.

Treat as hostile enemies: attack roll +4, Close Combat, Defense Value 7. A disabled drone stays down. They do not reset or recharge mid-encounter.

■ GM NOTE — THREAT PACING ON FLOOR 5

Not every room needs a drone encounter. Spread them deliberately — one or two activations as the party first enters deep zones, the main drone squad fight in END (see below). The hidden turrets should create paranoia and careful movement even in rooms where nothing activates. The feeling that something could open at any moment is worth more than constant contact.

TANKS — THE PRODUCTION FLOOR
PRIMARY FIND // TANKS MAP — 1927·3046

The tank room is enormous — ceiling higher than the floors above it have any right to allow. Twelve production tanks stand in two rows, each roughly the size of a shipping container, made of dark industrial steel with glass observation panels running their length. They are empty now, drained and sealed, but the infrastructure surrounding them is intact: pipe manifolds running floor to ceiling, monitoring consoles along the east wall still drawing minimal power, drainage channels cut into the floor connecting each tank to a central collection point. The scale of the room makes no sense for a research facility. It makes sense for a factory.

To the right of the tank rows: a cluster of heavy industrial pump housings connected by thick conduit that routes upward through the ceiling. These are the dispersal feeds — the mechanism by which whatever was produced in these tanks was moved into the building's ventilation and external dispersal systems. The pipes are clean. Whatever passed through them left no residue.

TECH TN 9

Reading the monitoring consoles. They log production cycles — twelve tanks, each cycled multiple times over approximately eight months. The terminology is opaque but the scale is not. The final cycle log shows all twelve tanks running simultaneously before the entry cuts off mid-record. No shutdown procedure was logged. They simply stopped.

INVESTIGATION TN 11

Tracing the dispersal pipes from the tank room upward. They do not connect to the building's standard ventilation. They route to a separate system — larger diameter, purpose-built — that terminates somewhere above Floor 1. Outside the building. High.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — SCALE COMPREHENSION TN 11

Any operative who stands in this room long enough to understand what it was built to produce — not what, but how much — must make a SPIKE Save at TN 11. The tanks are not research equipment. They are industrial production infrastructure. Whatever was made here was made in quantity sufficient to matter at a scale the party is only beginning to understand.

COMMAND — REEVES' OBSERVATION DECK
PRIMARY FIND // COMMAND MAP — 2584·7031

A large open room that reads immediately as a monitoring space rather than a laboratory. Eight workstations arranged in two rows face a raised command desk at the north wall — Reeves' desk, set apart from the others by its position, its size, and the fact that it has been meticulously cleared. Whatever was on it when the facility went dark, it was filed away before he left. Above the command desk: a bank of six screens mounted to the wall. The central screen is shattered — a starburst fracture pattern surrounded by impact marks. The flanking screens are intact, dark, still drawing minimal emergency power.

The room feels like something happened here. Not violence, exactly — it is too ordered for violence. But something deliberate occurred at that desk, and something less deliberate occurred at that screen.

INVESTIGATION TN 9 // THE BROKEN SCREEN

The damage to the central screen is a mix of two distinct types. The outer fractures — radiating from a central point — are consistent with a hard blunt impact, possibly a fist or a thrown object. The surrounding marks are bullet holes: three of them, tight grouping, fired from the direction of the desk at close range. The bullet holes came first — the pattern suggests they were fired deliberately into the screen's center. The blunt impact came after. Someone shot the screen, and then hit it.

There is no way to know who. There is no way to know why. What is clear is that whoever was at that desk at some point was not entirely composed when they left it.

■ FIND — RESEARCH JOURNALS

A shelf of numbered research volumes behind the command desk, neatly filed. Dense, technical, written for an audience with years of shared context. What comes through reading them is not a legible arc but a voice: certain, measured, entirely without doubt or remorse. Terminology is unexplained. Outcomes are noted without sentiment. The journals convey the shape of the man more clearly than any specific fact they contain.

Any operative spending time with the journals may make an Investigation TN 11 check. Success yields a single legible fragment — not an explanation, but a phrase, a measurement, a term that will mean something later. The GM should pick one that fits what has already been found without adding clarity. The journals are not a confession. They are a window into a mind.

■ FIND — THE ITALY FILE

A single manila folder labeled CALAFIORI — LIGURIA. Inside: two photographs of an isolated research station in the hills — bare concrete, narrow windows, surrounded by scrubland. An address outside Genoa. A handwritten note from Reeves:

"Incendiary approach — theoretically sound, practically irreversible. His conclusions re: emotional cauterization are consistent with my own re: baseline dampening. Different vectors. Same destination."

The address is in the hills outside Genoa, Italy. That region is largely uninhabited post-war. Whatever is there now — or whoever survived — may still be findable.

■ FIND — ACTIVE WORKSTATIONS

Two of the eight workstations still have power. Their screens show monitoring feeds — static from dead cameras, mostly, but one feed resolves intermittently: a corridor on Floor 4, in night-vision green. The feed cuts in and out. When it resolves, the corridor is empty. When it cuts back in a second later, something has moved in the dark.

INCINERATOR
FLOOR 5 SOUTH // INCINERATOR MAP — 5190·8263

Industrial waste disposal. Sealed. The incinerator itself is a large, institutional-grade unit — the kind used for medical and biological waste. It has been used and cleaned, repeatedly. The inside surfaces are scored. Whatever passed through here left no trace.

There are no documents, no labeling, no finds. The room communicates entirely through what is absent. Something that could not be kept was disposed of here. The party can speculate. They will not know what.

■ GM NOTE

Do not answer questions about what was incinerated. Let the room do its work. The scale of the unit is the information — it is far larger than any standard lab waste disposal. The scoring on the interior surfaces suggests repeated, heavy use. That is all the party gets.

ENCLOSURES — THE PS WING
PRIMARY FIND // ENCLOSURES MAP — 3847·6015

The enclosure room is larger than any other space on Floor 5. Twenty glass-and-steel cages arranged in a four-by-five grid fill most of the floor — each roughly two metres square, floor to ceiling, framed in heavy industrial steel with glass panels on all four sides. Two large cylindrical columns rise from the floor at the north end, reaching the ceiling: not structural supports but active infrastructure, connected by thick conduit to the cage array. Wiring traces across the floor in visible runs, connecting each cage to its neighbours and back to a central monitoring terminal positioned mid-room. A single door at the south end is the only entrance.

The terminal is still drawing emergency power. Its screen shows a status grid — twenty cells, each labeled with a PS designation. Most read VACANT. Several read TERMINATED. One reads ACTIVE.

Several of the cages contain shapes. Not bodies in the conventional sense — what remains after five years in a sealed, climate-controlled environment without further intervention. Faint, settled, unmistakable once seen. The glass is intact. Nothing has disturbed them.

PS-08 is not in this room. The grid on the terminal shows PS-08 assigned to a separate wing — a single isolated chamber, accessed from a door in the east wall. The nameplate beside that door reads: MITCHELL, C. — PS-08 — PRIMARY CANDIDATE.

TECH TN 11 // CENTRAL TERMINAL

The terminal's logs are partially accessible. Subject intake dates, condition assessments at intervals, integration threshold readings recorded as plain numbers without units. The final entries for most subjects are terse: a reading, a date, the word TERMINATED in the status field. No cause recorded. No commentary. One entry, for PS-12, has a handwritten annotation beside the printed log: "Requested contact with family. Declined per protocol." The handwriting is not Reeves'.

INVESTIGATION TN 9 // THE COLUMNS

The two cylindrical columns at the north end are feed mechanisms — they connected to the cage array and delivered something to the subjects within. The feed lines are dry and have been for years, but residue in the column bases is consistent with an aerosolised compound. Whatever the subjects were exposed to, it came from here, delivered directly into each sealed cage on a controlled schedule.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — TERMINAL READING TN 11 // CAGE CONTENTS TN 9

Reading the terminal and understanding what the PS designations represent: TN 11. Standing at the glass of an occupied cage long enough to look directly at what is inside: TN 9. The second trigger is optional — the party can choose not to look. The terminal save is unavoidable for anyone who reads it to the end.

SECURITY — ENCLOSURES SNIPER TURRET
ENCOUNTER // LETHAL // HIDDEN

A single sniper-class turret is mounted in the upper northeast corner of the enclosure room, recessed into a housing that reads as part of the ventilation infrastructure. It covers the entire room with a clear line of sight — and in particular, the east wall passage leading to PS-08. It does not activate on general entry to the room. It activates when an operative moves within ten metres of the PS-08 passage door, or when the central terminal is accessed.

AWARENESS TN 15

Spotting the turret housing before it activates. The difficulty reflects its position and disguise — it is genuinely hard to see until the targeting laser appears. On activation it fires without warning: attack roll +5, Heavy weapon (margin +4), Defense Value 10. It tracks across the full room. Cover behind the cage frames reduces its effective attack bonus by 2 but does not block it entirely — the gaps between cage panels are wide enough for a firing solution.

TECH TN 13 // SECURITY ROOM OVERRIDE

If the party already disabled Floor 5 turrets via the security room terminal near Entry, this turret is inactive. If not, it must be dealt with directly — destroyed (12 HP, Defense Value 10) or bypassed. Bypassing requires either exceptional stealth (Stealth TN 14, Disadvantage if already in the turret's acquisition arc) or using the cage grid as deliberate cover to approach the east wall from an angle outside the turret's optimal line. Neither is easy. Both are possible.

■ GM NOTE

This turret exists because Reeves protected PS-08 more than anything else on this floor. Whatever Mitchell was to the research, his chamber warranted a dedicated lethal deterrent that the other nineteen cages did not. Players who notice this asymmetry are noticing something real. Do not explain it. Let the question sit.

■ PS-08 — THE ISOLATED CHAMBER

The door in the east wall opens into a short passage ending at a single chamber — the same glass-and-steel construction as the grid cages, but larger, standing alone. The glass door is intact, sealed. The chamber beyond is visible through the glass: a cot, standard monitoring equipment, and nothing else except in the far corner near the floor: a mostly decomposed body. It is unidentifiable. The state of decomposition is consistent with five years in a sealed, climate-controlled environment. Multiple impact marks in the wall above the cot — visible through the glass from the passage. The body has not left the chamber. Neither, as far as can be determined, did anything else.

The identity of the body is intentionally unresolvable. Do not offer clarification. The nameplate says Mitchell. The body cannot be identified. Both facts are true. Let them sit together.

■ FIND — FLOOR ETCHING

In the corner of PS-08, opposite the body, scratched directly into the concrete floor with something small and sharp. Done carefully, in measured letters, not in panic.

even at the end of the world, humanity cannot resist a flair for the dramatic

The only other markings in the floor are forty-two tick marks arranged in rows, as though counting days. No signature anywhere.

■ GM NOTE — GM EYES ONLY

This is Mitchell's line — he says it in Hardware. Its presence here confirms he was in this chamber. Whether the body is his is a question the players cannot answer from this room, and should not be able to. Do not hint either way. Hold both facts — the name, the unidentifiable body — and let the ambiguity work. It will resolve in Hardware, not here.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — PS-08 ENTRY TN 11

Entering PS-08's chamber: optional save for any operative. The trigger is the combination — the nameplate, the sealed glass, the body visible before the door is even opened, the impact marks in the wall, the etching in the corner. Each detail is individually manageable. Together, they accumulate into something the mind cannot comfortably file.

ENCOUNTER — DRONE SQUAD
MINIBOSS // MELEE ONLY

When the party enters the END room and approaches the dispersal manifold or the tunnel door, the three dormant drones in their cradles activate simultaneously. These are full-size high-altitude dispersal units — larger than the proximity drones encountered elsewhere on Floor 5, built for payload carrying across long distances. In the confined space of the END room they cannot reach operational altitude, which makes them erratic and dangerous in a different way: they careen between the ceiling and the floor, using their mass and rotors as weapons. They attack exclusively through ramming charges and rotor-strike impacts.

Dispersal Drone (×3): HP 18, Defense Value 8, attack roll +4, Heavy melee (margin +4), Armor 2. Cannot be hacked mid-combat — their activation severs the control signal. Trait: Relentless — when reduced to 0 HP, the drone makes one final ramming attack before shutting down.

The tunnel door is accessible during the fight — the party can choose to disengage and run. The drones do not pursue beyond the END room. This is a meaningful option and should be available without penalty.

■ GM NOTE — TONE OF THE FIGHT

This should feel earned, not arbitrary. The party has descended through five floors of mounting dread, found the most significant evidence in the campaign, and is now standing in the room where the world was changed. The drones are a physical expression of Reeves' thoroughness — everything on Floor 5 has a failsafe. Run this fight with weight. Let it hurt. Let the tunnel door be a real temptation. Whatever the party does here, they leave the room different.

■ THE TUNNEL EXIT

The concrete passage slopes upward for approximately 80 meters, emerging through a camouflaged hatch in the hillside roughly 200 meters northeast of the facility's main structure — outside the fence line, in treeline. The hatch is unlocked from both sides. This is how Reeves left RE-01. The passage shows signs of one-time use: boot prints in the dust, partially preserved. One set, leading out. None leading back.

08 // EXTRACTION & DEBRIEF
LEAVING RE-01

The party exits the way they came. The turrets track them through the yard. The birdsong resumes the moment they clear the fence line. The silence of the facility is immediately behind them and immediately absent.

The drive back to Fort Wayne is however long the players need it to be. If anyone says something, let them. If no one says anything, let that stand too. Both are correct responses to Floor 5.

TOMMY GARZA — IF THEY RETURN

Tommy doesn't ask what they found. He asks about Brandon. Whatever the party tells him, his response is the same: he processes it and then pours them a drink without asking if they want one. "He was a good man before." He said that already. He says it again. It means something different now.

REMOTE REPORT TO WALKER

The party contacts Walker via short-range transmission from outside the facility's jamming radius. They give a brief summary — lower floors discovered, documentation recovered, personnel situation encountered. Walker receives it professionally. "Understood. Return to Copenhagen. We'll schedule a full debrief." Nothing more. He schedules it the way he schedules everything.

■ GM NOTE

This exchange should feel like filing a report with a machine. The party has just seen the room where the world was changed. Walker says "understood" and books a conference room. The institutional indifference is the horror.

THE DEBRIEF — COPENHAGEN

Director Walker receives the report professionally. He thanks the party. He schedules a documentation review. He does not ask follow-up questions about the lower floors until prompted, and when prompted: "We'll need to flag that for a structural assessment team." He means it. He has no idea what he's flagging.

The journals, the dispersal log, the Calafiori file — all submitted. Walker reviews none of it in front of the party. It will go to Science Division. Garcia may receive it. That is a later session's thread.

Following the debrief, depending on the party's SPIKE levels, Walker may recommend — or quietly require — that operatives with elevated readings complete a round of sessions with the Division IV wellness team before their next commission. This is framed as standard post-field protocol, not punishment. The wellness team is pleasant, efficient, and entirely unremarkable. Whether operatives comply or find a way around it is a choice to discuss at the table.

09 // MILESTONE — THE FLIGHT HOME
■ MILESTONE TRIGGER // THE RETURN FLIGHT TO COPENHAGEN

The milestone occurs on the flight home — not after the debrief, not after rest, but in the air between Indiana and Denmark. The liminal space is intentional. The party is suspended between what they found and what comes next. They have time to think. They have no duties. They are not yet home.

Award the milestone when at least one of the following is true: a party member has articulated, in-character, what the dispersal log means. Or the conversation has turned to Italy. Or someone has said something suggesting they understand, even partially, that the world they serve may not be what they were told.

Each operative chooses one:

  • Gain a new Skill (Trained, +2)
  • Upgrade a Trained Skill to Specialized (+3)
  • Gain a Trait (see Player's Guide — max 6)
■ GM NOTE — HOW TO GIVE THE MILESTONE

Step outside the fiction for a moment. Tell them they've reached a milestone. Let them choose. Then return to the flight. Whatever conversation was happening — continue it. The milestone is a beat, not an ending. The flight lands. Campaign 2 is Italy. They don't know that yet.

THE HOOK FORWARD

The Calafiori file contains an address in the hills outside Genoa, in a region that is largely a post-war wasteland. The fire destroyed the building years before RE-01 went dark. Calafiori's research — incendiary therapy, "emotional cauterization" — was consistent with Reeves' approach by Reeves' own assessment. Something survived that fire, or someone did. Reeves knew it, and kept working anyway.

The players may pursue this themselves, citing the Italy file. If not, Garcia will make contact between sessions — a short message, nothing more: "The address in that file. The area is listed as uninhabited. A flyover confirms ruins, no active structures. You might still want to go."

The only access to floors 3–5 is via the barred stairwell door on Floor 2 (breach charge required to open from this side) or via the service elevator shaft (pry open the doors, climb down). The choice between these has downstream consequences — a breach charge is loud and carries through the floor.
  • Turret escalation: Floor 3 — rubber/concussive, visible, non-lethal. Floor 4 — live rounds, visible, lethal. Floor 5 — live rounds, hidden, lethal. The security room on Floor 5 near Entry contains a terminal that can disable all Floor 5 turrets (TECH TN 13), but only if actively investigated. Players who skip it deal with turrets room by room.
  • Drones on Floor 5 activate on proximity throughout the floor. They are melee-only — ramming and impact attacks, no weapons. The three larger dispersal drones in END activate together as a miniboss encounter when the party approaches the manifold or tunnel door. They do not pursue beyond the END room.
  • Command room is Reeves' observation and monitoring space, not his laboratory. His desk is there, his journals are there. The broken central screen shows both bullet holes (deliberate, fired from the desk) and blunt impact damage (less deliberate, came after). Neither is explained. Do not explain it.
  • The Calafiori site is an isolated research station in the hills of Liguria, Italy — not in the city of Genoa. The surrounding area is post-war wasteland. Whatever remains there is not easily accessible and not expected to be inhabited.
  • The tunnel exit in END leads to the hillside northeast of the facility, outside the fence line. One set of boot prints, leading out, none returning. This is how Reeves left. It is also the party's fastest exit if the drone fight goes badly.
  • The wand is a shared party resource for evaluating others. Operatives' own SPIKE levels are always known via implant without a roll. The wand is for reading NPCs, civilians, and potential hostiles.
  • Floor 5 spatial layout is a reference, not a blueprint. The GM overview map shows one possible arrangement. Rearrange zones as needed for pacing. The essential elements — Security chokepoint, Tanks, Command, Enclosures/PS-08, END — must all be present. How they connect is the GM's call.
