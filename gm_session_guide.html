00 // HOW TO RUN THIS
"The facility is an archaeological descent — not a lore museum."
■ CAMPAIGN 1 DESIGN PRINCIPLE
■ FOUNDATIONAL RULE

The players are uncovering fragments of something buried, not being briefed on a setting. Do not explain what they find. Let terminology precede meaning. Let evidence precede significance. Let atmosphere precede answers. Every discovery should raise a larger question than it resolves.

WHAT PLAYERS LEAVE WITH

Nothing in RE-01 should confirm the central mystery. Players should leave Campaign 1 with dread, partial understanding, and a strong reason to go to Italy. They should not leave understanding what the Reset is, how it works, or who caused it.

MAPS IN PLAY

Player-facing maps have been created for each area. Reference them during play and coordinate finds and encounters with the room layouts shown. Maps are: Approach, Floor 1, Floor 2, Floor 3, Floor 4, and Floor 5 sub-maps: Command, Corridor, Enclosures, Incinerator, Power, Security, Surgery, Tanks, END. The END map is the crux — that is where the dispersal occurred.

■ THE HARDEST THING TO EXECUTE

The instruction to "not explain what they find" is the most important and the hardest. When players find terminology they don't understand — PS-series, semantic continuity, Hardware, recalibration — resist the pull to clarify. The strangeness is the information. Exposition is a last resort. Atmosphere is the primary tool.

01 // THE BRIEFING
SETTING // UGD DIV IV — COPENHAGEN, DENMARK // YEAR 5

The UGD's administrative headquarters occupies the former Ørestad district — rebuilt from the ground up rather than reclaimed from ruin. Orderly, functional, modern in the way that a diagram is modern. The party arrives separately and meets in a fourth-floor conference room.

Outside: floor-to-ceiling windows. The city looks like a proof of concept for civilization. Everything works. Nobody looks happy. Nobody looks unhappy. People walk between buildings as though completing tasks.

■ WHAT THE PARTY IS TOLD

"Research Establishment 01 was a Division IV-funded behavioral sciences facility in the former state of Indiana, United States. It was documented as pursuing aerosol therapy trials for SPIKE management and neurological treatment research. We have floor records for the upper two levels. The facility went dark at Year 0 and has not been reoccupied. Exterior scans indicate residual automated systems are still drawing on emergency power. Your objective is recovery and assessment: surviving documentation, structural status, outstanding risk factors. Return with whatever the facility contains."

If asked about alternative names for the facility: "That's the reference code. We've standardized."

DIVISION IV OPERATIONS OFFICER // COPENHAGEN HQ
SPIKE 0 — SERENE

What he is: A career operations officer who processes field assignments the way a machine processes inputs. He believes RE-01 is a recovery mission. He is not lying about this. He does not know there is anything else to believe.

On the lower floors: "The plans we have are the plans we have. Floor 2 shows a maintenance access point. Standard sub-basement infrastructure. Nothing flagged."

On finding personnel inside: "Handle with discretion." He does not elaborate.

PETRA GARCIA
SCIENCE DIVISION TECHNICAL LIAISON
SPIKE 1 — STABLE

Early 30s. Takes notes on paper, not terminals. Watches the room more than she contributes to it. Present to answer technical questions about the official research mandate — she does so competently and does not expand beyond the question.

THE SLIP

Toward the end of the briefing, she sets a printed page in front of one party member and taps it twice with one finger. It lists:

REEVES, E. (US) // CALAFIORI, M. (Italy) // QI, L. (China) // GUMEDE, A. (South Africa)

If asked what it is: "The four researchers commissioned at the same time as RE-01. Background. In case it's useful." She doesn't say why it might be useful. She doesn't know why, exactly. Something about the mission sits at an angle she can't name.

02 // FORT WAYNE
SETTING // FORMER INDIANA, USA — EN ROUTE TO RE-01

Fort Wayne is the closest settlement to Diamond Hill — the local name for RE-01's site. It functions. Distribution center, community hall, working infrastructure. People living their lives with consistent, moderate efficiency.

Something is off about it. Not wrong, exactly. Just quieter than it should be, given how many people are present.

Two people at a café table are having a conversation. Their voices are normal. Nobody laughs. Not because anything sad is happening. Because nothing is funny enough to produce that particular response. A child stops at the memorial kiosk, reads three names, and continues walking — because that is what the child knows to do with such a list.

THE COMMUNITY HALL
INTEL // ATMOSPHERE

Social space. Conversations are pleasant, specific, and almost entirely practical. One man is telling a story. When he reaches the end, listeners agree it was a good story. Nobody is delighted. The correct response has occurred.

Tommy Garza tends bar. His information about Diamond Hill is the most useful the party will get before entering.

CAROL HENSLEY
CIVILIAN RESIDENT // MEMORIAL SQUARE
SPIKE 3 — SHAKEN

Mid-40s. Standing near the memorial kiosk with the particular stillness of someone waiting for something they've forgotten they're waiting for. She doesn't know her readings are elevated. She checks her wand rarely.

"My son's name is on this panel. Danny. He was eight years old, according to the records I have. I know that we lived together. I know he was mine. My journals from before are very detailed about him — drawings he made, things he said. I've read them many times."

A pause.

"They're quite clear about how I felt. I can see it in the handwriting, in the way I wrote his name. I can't locate that feeling. I've been coming here trying to find it. I don't know what I think will happen."

If anyone stays and listens: "I understand this is a normal outcome. Everyone lost someone. Most people seem to have moved forward. I'm not sure why I keep coming back here."

She is not asking for anything. She will thank whoever spoke to her — with careful precision, not warmth — and turn back to the kiosk.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER // GM DISCRETION

A party member who engages with Carol honestly — not to gather information, but as a human being speaking to another human being — may make a SPIKE Save at TN 9. The trigger isn't the content. It's the gap: the distance between what she's describing and what she should be feeling, and what that says about the world they live in.

TOMMY GARZA
BARTENDER // COMMUNITY HALL
SPIKE 1 — STABLE

50s. Large. The kind of person who has stayed in one place while the world changed around him.

"Diamond Hill. Yeah. People went in, first couple years. Shelter-seekers, mostly — the facility was intact, had power, walls that held. Some came back elevated, bad elevated. We stopped tracking them after a while."

On recent activity: "There was a group, three years ago. Five of them. One of them never came back. Brandon. Good man, before. Nobody who's gone in there has made it out since — not that I know of. I've stood at the fence a few times. Tried calling for him. Nothing comes back." He lets the silence hold, then moves on.

He offers the party an extra round before they leave. He doesn't explain why.

03 // THE APPROACH
SETTING // RE-01 — RURAL MORGAN COUNTY, FORMER INDIANA

Forty minutes out of Fort Wayne, the road deteriorates — cracked asphalt, weeds splitting through, overhanging branches closing overhead. The facility appears gradually: first the fence line through the trees, then the bulk of the main structure above it.

The birdsong stops approximately 200 meters from the perimeter. There is no obvious reason for this.

THE PERIMETER FENCE

Chain-link, topped with wire. Electrification dead — the fence itself can be cut or climbed. However: the main entrance is protected by two active turret posts, still drawing on emergency power. Rubber/concussive rounds, non-lethal, but a direct hit suppresses and disorients. They track movement above the knee across the entrance approach. There is no secondary entrance — this is the only way in.

The fence has been repaired multiple times along the north side — new sections spliced into old, four different attempts visible, each cut from the inside.

TECH TN 11

The turrets can be disabled by severing their power feed. The conduit runs along the exterior of the building to a junction box behind the east turret post. A successful roll bypasses both simultaneously. Failure triggers a burst — the operative nearest the entrance takes a hit.

THE MAIN ENTRANCE

The primary entrance door is unlocked — the electronic lock lost power years ago. The door opens into the administrative vestibule connecting to Floor 1.

AWARENESS TN 9

The door frame has scratch marks at varying heights — concentrated around mid-chest, then lower, as though someone checked this door regularly for a long time. The most recent marks are closer to the ground.

RESEARCH ARCHIVE

Letters referencing a research station in the hills outside Genoa, Italy — an isolated site, not in the city itself. The tone escalates from collegial to heated over the course of several exchanges. Found in a separate locked cabinet (TECH TN 11 or keycard from this floor's office). Multiple letters and manifests referencing AP Logistics — a private supply company that arranged shipments to RE-01 for over a year. The tone is businesslike but the content is strange: the manifests describe HVAC components, but the weights and dimensions match no HVAC equipment in the building.

"The timeline cannot slip further. The WHO sessions are not going to produce anything we can use. If you want this to happen, it happens on our schedule."

Reeves' draft response is handwritten in the margin: "Agreed. Initiating." The name Prater means nothing to the players. The crates in the building do not contain HVAC components.

BRANDON COLE
CIVILIAN // TRAPPED IN RE-01 — THREE YEARS
SPIKE 10–11 — SPIKED / CONSUMED

He is not an NPC to interview. He is a person in the final stages of neurological collapse, and a physical threat. When the party locates him — anywhere on Floor 4 — he recognizes human presence and destabilizes immediately. He is fast. He is not rational. He does not want to communicate; he wants the stimulus to stop.

"GET AWAY — GET AWAY GET AWAY —"

"NO — STOP — I CAN'T — YOU'RE NOT — STOP —"

"YOU WEREN'T SUPPOSED TO — I TOLD THEM — WHY ARE YOU IN HERE —"

"PLEASE — PLEASE JUST — I CAN'T — PLEASE —"

His words carry no useful information. He is not hiding anything. He simply does not have coherent memory anymore. The sounds he makes are the sounds of a person who has been alone in a dark building for three years with nothing but the weight of everything a human being is supposed to feel and no way to process any of it.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — PROXIMITY TN 11 (DISADVANTAGE IF SPIKE 5+)

Any party member at SPIKE 5+ within 10 meters of Brandon must make a SPIKE Save at the start of their turn. This is emotional contagion — proximity to someone at this level is dangerous. He will also attack if operatives close within CLOSE range. Treat as a hostile encounter at GM discretion — he is not an enemy, but he cannot distinguish friend from threat.

GROUNDING BRANDON

Possible but extremely difficult. d20 + MIND vs TN 14, at Disadvantage. One attempt per operative. Success produces approximately 60–90 seconds of fractured, barely coherent calm. He does not know anything useful. He has forgotten most of what he saw. He cannot tell them about the tanks, the drones, the crates, or the lower floors — those facts have dissolved. What he can do is stop screaming, long enough for someone to look at him clearly. He looks back. That is all.

He will not leave. He cannot be helped here. The party should understand this by the time they find him, even if they don't say it out loud.

COMMAND — REEVES' LABORATORY
PRIMARY FIND // COMMAND MAP

A private workspace set apart from the production floor by a glass partition — floor to ceiling, clean, intact. Reeves' desk is ordered. His work is filed. The man was methodical to the end.

Twelve volumes. The early volumes describe legitimate research. The later volumes describe the Reset — not by that name. He calls it THE RECALIBRATION. The language is technical and proud. ENCLOSURES — THE PS WING
PRIMARY FIND // ENCLOSURES MAP

A long corridor of twenty chambers, numbered PS-01 through PS-20 on the enclosures map. Each chamber is approximately four meters square, sealed with heavy glass doors. Observation windows. Interior monitoring equipment, still drawing minimal power. Each chamber has a subject nameplate mounted beside the door. The chambers contain simple cots — no desks, no furnishings beyond the bare minimum. PS-01 through PS-07 and PS-09 through PS-20 are empty — cleaned, sealed, unremarkable except for the scale of their containment infrastructure, which is far beyond what any aerosol therapy trial would require.

References to PS-08 in documents found on upper floors should orient the party toward the correct chamber.

PS-08 is not empty.

■ PS-08

The nameplate reads: MITCHELL, C. — PS-08 — PRIMARY CANDIDATE.

The glass door is broken from the inside — shattered outward into the corridor. The chamber beyond contains a simple cot, standard monitoring equipment, and nothing else.

In the far corner of the chamber, near the floor: there is a mostly decomposed body. It is unidentifiable. The state of decomposition is consistent with five years' time in a sealed, climate-controlled environment. Multiple bullet holes in the wall above the cot. The glass was broken outward. The body is inside.

The identity of the body is intentionally unresolvable. Do not offer clarification. The nameplate says Mitchell. The body cannot be identified. Both facts are true. Let them sit together.

■ FIND — FLOOR ETCHING

In the corner of the chamber, opposite the body, scratched directly into the concrete floor with something small and sharp. It appears to have been done hastily.

even at the end of the world, humanity cannot resist a flair for the dramatic

The only other etchings in the floor are fourty-two tick marks, seemingly signifying days. No signature anywhere.

■ SPIKE TRIGGER — PS-08 ENTRY TN 11

Entering the chamber: optional save for any operative. The trigger is the combination: the nameplate, the body, the bullet holes, the broken glass from the inside, the etching in the corner. Each detail is individually manageable. Together, they accumulate into something the mind cannot comfortably file.

08 // EXTRACTION & DEBRIEF
LEAVING RE-01

The party exits the way they came. The turrets track them through the yard. The birdsong resumes the moment they clear the fence line. The silence of the facility is immediately behind them and immediately absent.

The drive back to Fort Wayne is however long the players need it to be. If anyone says something, let them. If no one says anything, let that stand too. Both are correct responses to Floor 5.

TOMMY GARZA — IF THEY RETURN

Tommy doesn't ask what they found. He asks about Brandon. Whatever the party tells him, his response is the same: he processes it and then pours them a drink without asking if they want one. "He was a good man before." He said that already. He says it again. It means something different now.

REMOTE REPORT TO WALKER

The party contacts Walker via short-range transmission from outside the facility's jamming radius. They give a brief summary — lower floors discovered, documentation recovered, personnel situation encountered. Walker receives it professionally. "Understood. Return to Copenhagen. We'll schedule a full debrief." Nothing more. He schedules it the way he schedules everything.

■ GM NOTE

This exchange should feel like filing a report with a machine. The party has just seen the room where the world was changed. Walker says "understood" and books a conference room. The institutional indifference is the horror.

THE DEBRIEF — COPENHAGEN

Director Walker receives the report professionally. He thanks the party. He schedules a documentation review. He does not ask follow-up questions about the lower floors until prompted, and when prompted: "We'll need to flag that for a structural assessment team." He means it. He has no idea what he's flagging.

The journals, the dispersal log, the Calafiori file — all submitted. Walker reviews none of it in front of the party. It will go to Science Division. Garcia may receive it. That is a later session's thread.

Following the debrief, depending on the party's SPIKE levels, Walker may recommend — or quietly require — that operatives with elevated readings complete a round of recalibration sessions with the Division IV wellness team before their next commission. This is framed as standard post-field protocol, not punishment. The wellness team is pleasant, efficient, and entirely unremarkable. Whether operatives comply or find a way around it is a choice to discuss at the table.

09 // MILESTONE — THE FLIGHT HOME
■ MILESTONE TRIGGER // THE RETURN FLIGHT TO COPENHAGEN

The milestone occurs on the flight home — not after the debrief, not after rest, but in the air between Indiana and Denmark. The liminal space is intentional. The party is suspended between what they found and what comes next. They have time to think. They have no duties. They are not yet home.

Award the milestone when at least one of the following is true: a party member has articulated, in-character, what the dispersal log means. Or the conversation has turned to Italy. Or someone has said something suggesting they understand, even partially, that the world they serve may not be what they were told.

Each operative chooses one:

  • Gain a new Skill (Trained, +2)
  • Upgrade a Trained Skill to Specialized (+3)
  • Gain a Trait (see Player's Guide — max 6)
■ GM NOTE — HOW TO GIVE THE MILESTONE

Step outside the fiction for a moment. Tell them they've reached a milestone. Let them choose. Then return to the flight. Whatever conversation was happening — continue it. The milestone is a beat, not an ending. The flight lands. Campaign 2 is Italy. They don't know that yet.

THE HOOK FORWARD

The Calafiori file contains an address in the hills outside Genoa, in a region that is largely a post-war wasteland. The fire destroyed the building years before RE-01 went dark. Calafiori's research — incendiary therapy, "emotional cauterization" — was consistent with Reeves' approach by Reeves' own assessment. Something survived that fire, or someone did. Reeves knew it, and kept working anyway.

The players may pursue this themselves, citing the Italy file. If not, Garcia will make contact between sessions — a short message, nothing more: "The address in that file. The area is listed as uninhabited. A flyover confirms ruins, no active structures. You might still want to go."

CONTINUITY NOTES FOR GM
  • PS-08 body is unidentifiable. The nameplate says Mitchell. The body cannot be identified. Do not disambiguate in play — the ambiguity is the point. Players should not be able to confirm or rule out Mitchell's fate from this room alone.
  • The comms jam is localized to the facility and the surrounding area. The facility has been low priority — no one had reason to actively scan for signal from this site. The jam presents as silence, not interference. The players should be the first to discover it.
  • Brandon Cole will not leave. This is final. If players attempt extraction by force, he becomes violent and retreats to the deeper maintenance runs. He has been here three years. He cannot be saved in this campaign.
  • Floors 3–5 are not on the UGD briefing documents. Walker doesn't know they exist. This is not a conspiracy on Walker's part — the plans he has are the plans he was given.
