01 // THE PHILOSOPHY OF SPIKE

SPIKE is a narrative-first sci-fi TTRPG built around player philosophy. The role of the GM is not to guide players to a correct outcome, but to construct situations where every outcome carries a meaningful cost.

PRESENT TRADE-OFFS, NOT PROBLEMS
Do not present problems with optimal solutions. Present problems with trade-offs. If players attempt to avoid the cost of a decision, the world should reinforce that avoidance is itself a choice with consequences. There is no "having both." If players try, introduce pressure that forces prioritization.
SHOW CONSEQUENCES BEFORE EXPLAINING THEM
Let players experience the world before revealing why it works that way. Make the cost visible in people, not systems. A stable world should feel emotionally hollow. A harsh world should feel brutally alive but unjust. Exposition is a last resort — atmosphere is your primary tool.
REFLECT PLAYER VALUES BACK AT THEM
NPCs, factions, and outcomes should echo the logic of the party's past decisions. If they acted ruthlessly, the world around them begins to feel ruthlessly logical. If they acted with compassion, the world offers fragile hope. Players shape the fiction — the GM reflects it.
REMOVE THE ILLUSION OF A PERFECT OUTCOME
Every meaningful choice in SPIKE costs something. Victory is not the standard. The standard is: what did you preserve, and what did you sacrifice to do it? Players who reach the end of SPIKE should feel the weight of those answers — not the satisfaction of having avoided them.
WHAT YOU ARE RUNNING

SPIKE is a psychological thriller and mystery. It rewards players who ask questions, who sit with uncertainty, and who let things land slowly. Sessions will not always have clear climaxes or tidy resolutions. A session might end mid-corridor, mid-conversation, mid-realisation. This is intentional.

The horror, when it comes, is quiet before it is loud. The game rewards paying attention to the things that feel slightly wrong before anything feels dramatically wrong. Trust the design. The arc works.

02 // CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE
THE SHAPE OF THE CAMPAIGN

SPIKE is built in five interconnected campaigns. Each asks a different philosophical question. Each answer is shaped by the decisions made in the one before. The campaigns accumulate — small things compound into something large. The endgame question cannot be reached without traveling through everything that preceded it.

CAMPAIGN 1 // INDIANA, USA // YEAR 5
SPIKE: THE REEVES TRIALS
WHAT HAPPENED? WHAT'S WRONG?
Players are UGD operatives investigating a defunct research lab in Indiana. They find research logs, evidence of human testing, SPIKED scavengers, and an undeniable link to Calafiori's lab in Italy. They uncover information they do not yet understand. The picture is incomplete by design. Let the mystery breathe.

Why now: A worldwide SPIKE Storm approximately one week before Session 1 forced the UGD to confront that they do not understand how the Reset works. Five years of peace had made the RE-01 file easy to deprioritize. The Storm made it urgent overnight. The party's commission was expedited. Walker won't frame it as panic — he doesn't have the emotional register for panic — but the speed of the paperwork is notable if anyone thinks to ask.
CAMPAIGN 2 // ITALY // YEAR 6
SPIKE: WHISPERED ECHOES
WHAT DID WE LOSE?
The party discovers Calafiori's incendiary methods and the Purified. They face a pivotal choice: give this information to the UN (feeding the Control), or destroy it (at great personal SPIKE cost). Both paths cost something irreplaceable. The campaign ends with the team returning to the UN, changed.
CAMPAIGN 3 // SOUTH AFRICA // YEAR 6–7
SPIKE: DRONE REBELLION
WHAT'S BREAKING?
Awakening becomes possible for the first time. The nanite mesh begins to fail in targeted areas of Asia — the Control's controlled experiment. Gumede's surviving drones reveal the true scope of pre-Reset cooperation. This is the first domino pointing toward global collapse.
CAMPAIGN 4 // CHINA // YEAR 7
SPIKE: THE LOST TRANSMISSION
WHERE'S OUR PATH FORWARD?
The party seeks Dr. Qi — alive, Reset, and serving as archivist of his own dead lab. His satellites contain data pointing to the Control's location. A SPIKE Storm offers a moment of fractured lucidity. Under the right conditions, Qi can be Awakened. The campaign ends with the revelation of the lost transmission — the players transition into Hardware.
PREQUEL // THE ROCKIES, USA // 2048
SPIKE: HARDWARE
HOW DID WE CAUSE THIS?
Players create new characters and live the events of the lost transmission. A team dispatched by the UGD Chairman infiltrates Reeves' lab. They find the true nature of the Reset and face the Mitchell triad: FREE, LEAVE, or TERMINATE. This decision determines who becomes the Control and shapes everything in SPIKE: CURE.
CAMPAIGN 5 / FINALE // MOTO NUI / POINT NEMO
SPIKE: CURE
WHAT DO WE DO NOW?
Dual finale path based on Hardware's outcome. Branch R (Reeves) and Branch P (Prater) present different final antagonists, different world-states, and different versions of the final question. The finale is not about winning. It is about what your players believe should exist.
03 // EXECUTION GUIDELINES
HARDWARE // THE PREQUEL

Hardware establishes the party's moral baseline through decisive action. Players must act as separate characters — they are not extensions of their current campaign selves. They don't know what they're building toward.

The choice at the end of Hardware (FREE / LEAVE / TERMINATE) is the most consequential decision in the campaign. Make sure the weight is felt without telegraphing that it's a fork. They should not know they are choosing the final antagonist.

CURE // THE FINALE

Cure presents a world shaped by Hardware. The final conflict reflects and challenges the values demonstrated in the prequel. Players who were ruthless in Hardware will encounter a world that has grown ruthless in return — and must decide if they will reinforce it or reject it.

Players who were compassionate will encounter a world offering fragile hope — and must decide if the safety is worth what it cost.

THE RESET AS ROLEPLAY CONSTRAINT

Post-Reset characters have limited emotional access. They know facts about their pasts but feel no personal connection. Trauma, passion, and deep love are absent. Relationships are pleasant but shallow. Conflict fizzles quickly. This is the setup. SPIKE is the payoff.

When a character's SPIKE rises and something breaks through — when they experience something they have absolutely no category for — that is one of the most interesting things you can play. The Reset is not a cage. It is the dramatic tension.

As GM: do not rush to resolve emotional moments. Let them be strange. Let them be slow. The game is built around that arc — trust it.

SPIKED AS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY

When a PC reaches SPIKE 10, it is a person in crisis — not a villain reveal. They are experiencing every emotion they were never taught to manage, all at once, at full intensity, with no framework. This is not fun for the character.

Hold the moment as a table. The Behavior Table gives mechanics. The roleplay gives meaning. Lean into the tragedy. It can be powerful and even beautiful if the table holds it well. CONSUMED (11) should always be discussed with the player first.

PLAYER VS. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE

There will be moments where players understand something their characters do not. This is a feature. The dramatic tension of knowing something your character can't fully articulate is one of the most powerful tools available.

Do not rush players to give their characters player-level understanding. Let the characters earn it at their own pace. The gap between what you know and what they know is where the story happens.

FAILURE AS STORY ENGINE

A failed roll that costs something — a raised SPIKE, a lost resource, a new complication — generates more story than a clean success. The worst moments for characters are often the best moments for the game.

Reward meaningful failure with narrative momentum. A complication on a Nat 1 is not punishment — it is an invitation. Design the invitation thoughtfully.

GUMEDE — COOPERATION BEFORE OPPOSITION

Dr. Gumede's arc is a character beat that must be held clearly in prep, because the evidence players find in Campaign 1 (a sanctioned nanite shipment from her lab to RE-01) could read as straightforwardly sinister if the GM doesn't know the full shape of it.

The timeline: Gumede cooperated with Reeves early in the research period, including a UGD-sanctioned transfer of 600 nanite units to RE-01. This was legitimate collaborative research at the time. As the true direction of Reeves' work became apparent, and after a horrified subordinate within Calafiori's lab made direct contact with her sharing what she knew, Gumede turned against him — ultimately directing her own robots to destroy her facility rather than allow her technology to reach him.

Her early cooperation is not hypocrisy. It is the shape of how people get drawn into things before they understand what they're drawn into, and then act at great cost when they do. The nanite shipment players find in Campaign 1 is a breadcrumb pointing forward to this. In Campaign 3, when Gumede's drone recordings surface her full arc, that earlier find should land differently than it did the first time.

LORE CONTINUITY — GM PREP NOTES

PS-08 and the Hardware dome: PS-08 in RE-01 is Charles Mitchell's pre-Reset trial containment space within Reeves' facility. The body inside cannot be identified. Keep these two things clearly separate in your own prep: PS-08 is a distinct location from whatever containment Mitchell occupied during Hardware. Players should not receive confirmation that the body is Mitchell's — or that it isn't. Both facts (the nameplate bearing his name, the unidentifiable body) should be allowed to sit together unresolved. The ambiguity is intentional and will not be resolved until Hardware.

The UGD Chairman's disappearance: He was missing from Day 1 of Year 1, captured by the Control during the Quiet Period (the 5–7 days of global unconsciousness following the Reset). His absence was noted — he was one of tens of millions of missing persons in a world that had just lost its technological infrastructure for locating people. Some time passed before the absence was fully accepted and a new Chairman appointed. The Control has used his security credentials for backdoor UGD access ever since. If this surfaces in play, the framing is: his absence was real and acknowledged, just not urgently actionable given the scale of post-Reset missing persons.

04 // HARDWARE / CURE REFLECTION
THE MITCHELL TRIAD

The choice made at the end of Hardware — to FREE, LEAVE, or TERMINATE Charles Mitchell — determines the identity of the Control and the shape of CURE. The players will not know they are making this choice until it is made.

■ BRANCH R — REEVES AS CONTROL (TERMINATE or specific FREE outcomes)

Reeves survives and relocates to Point Nemo. He monitors humanity from a floating base, refining his work toward Reset 2.0. A perfectionist who cannot stand that his solution was imperfect — who manufactured a world where he will never receive the recognition he denies himself wanting.

World State: Reeves is conducting controlled experiments on the Awakened in Asia. He has deliberately accelerated mesh decay there to study effects. He wants to find the perfect Awakened candidate for a new Prime — possibly two Awakened who could produce a child. The chaos is his laboratory.

Cure Path: The final question becomes about perfection and consent. Is a perfect, involuntary peace worth more than an imperfect, chosen one? Among the Awakened at Reeves' facility are NPCs the party met in Drone Rebellion and The Lost Transmission. Some may agree with Reeves.

■ BRANCH P — PRATER AS CONTROL (LEAVE or specific TERMINATE outcomes)

Prater kills Reeves and seizes control. A prime opportunist who believes SPIKE is natural selection — that those who succumb to it simply cannot handle reality and deserve to filter out. He has been stockpiling weaponry, recruiting the Awakened by blaming the UGD, and deliberately causing SPIKE Storms through interference with the Prime.

World State: Prater is rerouting nanites from Asia using non-Reset scientists. The collapse there is not accidental — it is deliberate destabilization. He plans to recruit the survivors and overthrow the UGD using his accumulated arsenal. Once in power, he intends to let the "damned" tear themselves apart.

Cure Path: Players meet Prater at UGD headquarters. The final question becomes about who deserves to survive and who has the right to decide. Some of his logic will be internally consistent. That's the point.

TERMINATE (RUTHLESSNESS)

The world becomes harsh, selective, and survival-driven. Systems are efficient but cold.

Players must decide: Do they reinforce this system? Or reject it and risk total collapse?

FREE / LEAVE (COMPASSION)

The world offers the possibility of universal stability — at the cost of emotional depth and individuality.

Players must decide: Is safety worth the loss of what made them who they are?

05 // THE ENDGAME QUESTION
THE FINAL DECISION IN CURE

The final decision in Cure should not be about winning. It should be about answering — in front of each other, in character, with full knowledge of what it costs — three questions:

  • What is worth preserving?
  • What is acceptable to sacrifice?
  • What kind of imperfect world do your players believe should exist?

The available final choices in Cure all have costs. Assimilating as Primes theoretically awakens the entire network — but at what personal cost, and what does it mean to make that choice for all of humanity? Destroying the network causes SPIKE to return at full force with no gentle awakening. Doing nothing and walking away is also a choice — and the world will continue to suffer it. There is no optimal outcome. The design is working correctly if the players feel the weight of that.

A FINAL NOTE ON YOUR ROLE

You are not the storyteller. You are the world. The players are the storytellers — they are writing the answer to the questions SPIKE is asking. Your job is to make sure the questions are asked honestly, that the costs are real, and that the world responds to choices as a world would: slowly, imperfectly, and without mercy for the things people tried to avoid.

Make it worth the journey. Hold it well.
